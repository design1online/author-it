Choosing Amazon keywords & categories is a strategy.
While you were writing your book, you were making marketing decisions whether you knew it or not.
Here’s how to do it with intention
Know your genre (don’t “vibe” it)
If your book sits between genres, pick the primary reader expectation:
→ Romance = emotional payoff (HEA/HFN)
→ Thriller = tension + danger
→ Memoir = transformation + lived experience
Readers don’t browse “kind of everything.” They browse specific promises.
Tropes are your targeting system
Keywords aren’t just descriptors—they’re search behavior.
Think like a reader:
“Enemies to lovers”
“Found family”
“WW2 resistance”
“Addiction recovery memoir”
If readers can name it, they can search it.
Categories = positioning, not accuracy
Don’t just pick what your book is—pick where it can compete.
Too broad → you disappear
Too niche → no traffic
Look for categories where:
✔ Your book clearly fits
✔ Comparable books are selling
✔ The top ranks feel achievable
Stack your keywords with purpose
Mix:
Genre signals (“historical fiction WW2”, “cozy murder mystery”)
Trope signals (“forbidden love”, “second chance”)
Spice Levels for romance (“sweet, clean romance”, “spicy fantasy romance”)
Each keyword should answer: who is this for?
Study the top 100 (this is non-negotiable)
Your categories already have a blueprint:
What words repeat?
What tropes dominate?
What emotional promise is being made?
Don’t reinvent—align, then differentiate.
Avoid the biggest mistake
Trying to appeal to everyone.
Clear positioning > broad appeal
Specific sells.
—
If your keywords and categories are vague, your book is invisible.
If they’re intentional, you’re placing it exactly where the right readers are already looking.
And remember → none of these elements are set in stone. You can change your categories and keywords whenever you want.
The biggest rule: Don’t promise cake (what readers want) and serve them catfood (what they super don’t want). Don’t position your book in a category it doesn’t belong in (eg: don’t select romance categories if romance isn’t present in your book or is just a subplot).
You can do it!
